## 🚀 Como Fazer o Upload no Academia.edu (Fluxo Geral)

1. Faça login na sua conta em: **[https://www.academia.edu/](https://www.academia.edu/)**
2. No canto superior direito da tela inicial, clique no botão **"Upload"** (ou ícone de nuvem/seta para cima).
3. Selecione a opção **"Upload Paper"**.
4. Arraste ou selecione o arquivo PDF do artigo (veja os caminhos locais exatos abaixo).
5. Na tela de metadados que abrirá:
   - Cole o **Title** (Título exato em inglês).
   - Verifique o **Author** (Bruno L. Olivaes).
   - Cole o **Abstract** (Resumo em texto puro).
   - Digite e selecione as **Research Interests / Tags** (Áreas de conhecimento sugeridas).
   - Em **Publication Type**, selecione *Preprint* ou *Working Paper*.
   - Se já tiver gerado o DOI no Zenodo, insira no campo *DOI*.
6. Clique em **"Save & Finish"** (ou **"Post Paper"**).
7. Repita para cada um dos 5 artigos abaixo.

We present a pre-registered, radix-agnostic search for discrete scale invariance (DSI) in the Planck 2018 cosmic microwave background temperature power spectrum. DSI predicts a characteristic log-periodic modulation of primordial perturbations governed by the preferred scaling factor lambda, inducing oscillations in Fourier space with angular log-frequency omega = 2*pi/ln(lambda). Furthermore, we replicate our protocol on synthetic Large-Scale Structure mocks matching the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) survey precision, confirming identical null-detection behavior.

- **Abstract para Copiar:**
```text
Precision nanomechanical measurements of the Casimir interaction at sub-micron separations are increasingly audited for hypothetical short-range modifications of gravity and exotic forces. However, residual force curves are intrinsically non-uniformly sampled and contaminated by steep short-range baseline drifts. In this paper, we demonstrate how conventional data-processing choices inadvertently fabricate statistically overwhelming false positive signals or severely degrade experimental sensitivity. Specifically, we show that: (1) naive 3-sigma Gaussian thresholds applied to exponentially distributed periodograms flag up to 36 spurious peaks on pure white noise; (2) unphysical polynomial detrending across steep z^-4 short-range profiles induces Runge-type boundary ringing that generates an apparent oscillatory anomaly at lambda = 583.5 nm with an artificial significance of FAP = 1.49 x 10^-45; (3) cubic spline resampling on irregular grids (CV = 40.7%) destroys 57% of true signal variance at short wavelengths, degrading the 90% Minimum Detectable Level by a factor of 2.28; and (4) surface electrostatic patch potentials measured via Kelvin Probe Force Microscopy (KPFM) yield strictly monotonic power-law decays (P proportional to d^-2.29), proving that patch roughness cannot generate periodic residual modulations. Adopting a physically motivated inverse-power baseline collapses residual variance (chi^2/dof = 1.14) and restores the true experimental null result (FAP = 0.163).
```

Recent cosmological observations from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) Year 1 Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO) measurements, combined with Type Ia supernovae and Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) distance priors, exhibit a mild preference (~1.5sigma - 1.9sigma) for dynamical dark energy over a cosmological constant. - **Título:**  
  `When Harmonic ZNE Fails: A Comparative Benchmark of Error Mitigation on NISQ Devices`
- **Autor:**  
  `Bruno L. Olivaes`
- **Áreas / Tags no Academia.edu:**  
  `Quantum Computing`, `Quantum Information`, `Quantum Error Mitigation`, `Zero-Noise Extrapolation`, `Randomized Compiling`, `Qiskit`
- **Abstract para Copiar:**
```text
Zero-Noise Extrapolation (ZNE) is a cornerstone of quantum error mitigation on Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) processors. While standard polynomial and exponential extrapolation models assume monotonic error accumulation, coherent unitary noise—such as persistent Stark shifts and crosstalk over-rotations—induces oscillatory expectation trajectories. Recently, harmonic and spectral ZNE techniques leveraging Lomb-Scargle periodograms have been proposed to mitigate periodic phase errors. In this work, we present a pre-registered, head-to-head empirical benchmark comparing standard polynomial, exponential, and harmonic ZNE against two-qubit Clifford Pauli Twirling across 1-qubit Ramsey echo and 4-qubit Transverse-Field Ising Model (TFIM) Trotter circuits under strictly matched shot budgets (N_total = 24,000 shots per seed). In a 1-qubit coherent Ramsey sequence probed across deep noise scales (lambda <= 17), harmonic ZNE achieves an 85.4% reduction in Mean Absolute Error (MAE), overcoming sign inversions where polynomial and exponential models fail catastrophically. However, in an entangled 4-qubit Trotter circuit, harmonic ZNE fails catastrophically: its MAE surges to 2.28 under moderate coherent over-rotation (epsilon = 0.04 rad), performing significantly worse than unmitigated execution (MAE = 0.148). In contrast, randomized compiling via Pauli Twirling converts non-commuting coherent ZZ crosstalk into stochastic Pauli channels, enabling linear and exponential ZNE to achieve robust error reductions of 80.8% - 81.0%. We delineate the physical mechanisms governing this breakdown—multi-mode non-commutative interference and parameter ill-conditioning on bounded scale schedules—and establish operational guidelines for quantum hardware practitioners.
```

- **Abstract para Copiar:**
```text
The advent of quantum computing threatens classical public-key cryptography via Shor's algorithm, prompting the global migration toward Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) standardized by NIST in August 2024. While lattice-based cryptography is often assumed to impose severe computational overhead, our empirical benchmarks demonstrate the opposite: key generation in ML-KEM-768 (NIST FIPS 203) executes in just 41.3 microseconds—approximately 740x faster than RSA-2048 (30.49 ms). Instead, the true architectural bottleneck lies in network transport and wire payload expansion. While classical ECDH requires only 65 bytes per key share, ML-KEM-768 transmits a 1,184-byte public key and a 1,088-byte ciphertext (2,272 B total KEM wire), expanding to 2,402 B in hybrid deployments (X25519 + ML-KEM-768). Over standard Ethernet with a Maximum Segment Size (MSS) of 1,460 bytes, a full hybrid ClientHello flight (1,518 B) breaches the 1-MSS boundary, mandating TCP segmentation into two distinct packets. Through analytical modeling and empirical socket transport audits, we evaluate the impact against RFC 6928 initial congestion windows (initcwnd = 10). We demonstrate that while clean networks absorb multi-segment handshakes within the initial flight, lossy links suffer increased head-of-line blocking sensitivity and legacy middlebox inspection buffer drops.
```
